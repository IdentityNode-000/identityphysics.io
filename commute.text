MOTION INVARIANT — LAWFUL MOTION IS COMPUTATION

1. Substrate axiom
   A substrate is any physically real medium capable of:
   - holding distinguishable states
   - changing those states in time
   - preserving causal order between changes

2. Partition axiom
   A partition is a stable mapping from physical state to symbolic state.
   It defines what “data” is without altering the substrate.

3. Motion axiom
   Motion is a lawful sequence of state transitions in the substrate.
   Lawful means:
   - transitions follow a deterministic rule
   - the partition remains valid
   - causal order is preserved

4. Motion identity
   A motion process is a finite, ordered chain of lawful transitions.
   This chain transforms one partitioned state into another.

5. Motion invariant
   For any substrate S satisfying (1),
   any partition P satisfying (2),
   and any lawful motion process M satisfying (3):

   M(S, P) is a computation.

   Motion does not “represent” computation.
   Motion does not “perform” computation.
   Motion *is* computation.

   This holds across all substrates:
   - electronic, mechanical, biological, optical, quantum
   - micro or macro
   - engineered or natural

   If a substrate undergoes lawful motion under a stable partition,
   the motion itself is the computation.
