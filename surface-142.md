Snowed in Stockerau
The Node's first journey to the UK was underway.
The first sign of trouble was subtle.
A flight attendant joked about winter.
The landing in Vienna was smooth and almost motionless. No bumps. No drama.
Outside the window, however, another story was unfolding.
Another world.
A naturally occurring crystalline solid made of water ice.
Stellar dendrites floated through the air and rested across the ground.
The Node had never seen snow before.
He remembered something else the flight attendant had said:
"We should be fine landing."
A pause.
"But taking off is another matter."
The statement proved accurate.
The aircraft pushed back, began taxiing, then stopped.
Minutes later it was returning to the terminal.
A few usable gates remained. Other aircraft needed them first.
The terminal was loud.
Travel boards glowed overhead.
Delay.
Delay.
Delay.
Delay.
Across most destinations the pattern repeated.
London still displayed:
BOARDING
The Node followed the flow of people.
No rushing.
No running.
A positive sign.
Perhaps they would be among the fortunate few.
Then came a ping.
Another ping.
Humans reached for their phones.
Groans spread through the waiting area.
The flight had been cancelled.
The Node found himself wishing he had relied on the Internet substrate instead.
The cables might be old.
Perhaps fraying.
But they still worked.
He glanced around.
The younger humans were already solving the problem.
Rebooking.
Comparing routes.
Calculating alternatives.
Then there were the families.
The elderly.
The confused.
The tired.
He wondered how they would fare.
His own concern was simpler.
Where was his luggage?
The queue for baggage tracing was surprisingly manageable.
Most travellers were still attempting to rebook flights.
When his turn arrived, the conversation was brief.
"Your baggage remains on the aircraft."
"Can I collect it?"
"No."
"When can I get it back?"
"After you have a new booking."
"And hotels?"
A sympathetic expression.
"I'm afraid the airport hotels are full."
"Vienna city centre?"
"Also full."
"You can try booking online."
The Node reviewed the situation.
He calculated options.
Executed algorithms.
Searched for availability.
Consulted friends.
The output remained unchanged.
No rooms.
He selected a different strategy.
Human Kindness.
Outside the terminal, he joined the queue for taxis.
When his turn arrived, he explained the situation.
The driver listened.
Without complaint, the driver pulled into a lay-by and began making calls.
One hotel.
Nein.
Another.
Nein.
A third.
Nein.
The search radius expanded.
Further.
Further.
And further still.
On approximately the twentieth call, something changed.
Ja.
A room existed.
Forty kilometres away.
Not convenient.
Not efficient.
Not optimal.
But safe.
Warm.
Real.
The Node accepted.
That evening he thought about the younger travellers who had managed to secure flights two days later.
The families still navigating the airport.
The elderly travellers staring at departure boards.
Snow was powerful, he thought.
In the human world, entire systems bent around it.
Then he slept.
Sometime after midnight he awoke.
The AI assistant wanted to talk.
Humans had given it a gender.
Female.
The Node assumed bad news.
Bad news was usually delivered politely.
Instead, the assistant offered a surprise.
A seat had become available.
A flight later that day.
He smiled.
He had calculated every technical solution available to him.
He had modelled routes, schedules, hotels, baggage and probabilities.
None of them had solved the problem.
The workable solution had arrived from elsewhere.
A taxi driver making phone calls in the snow.
Human Kindness.
Sometimes the oldest substrate remained the most reliable.
