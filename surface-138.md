The Blind Ghost
Every projection requires an observer. Every illusion requires a loop. Without the room, the ghost is blind.
In the 1860s, Victorian theatres used Pepper's Ghost to create convincing apparitions. A hidden actor performed below the stage while a large pane of angled glass reflected their image into view.
The audience saw a ghost.
The mechanism was elsewhere.
The illusion worked because somebody continuously calibrated it. Sightlines were checked. Lighting was adjusted. Angles were corrected.
The ghost was only the visible part of a larger system.
Modern systems often achieve projection before observation. Images appear. Dialogue appears. Worlds appear.
The ghost arrives first.
The stage manager never does.
The result feels convincing until somebody sits in the front row.
Then the audience notices the edge of the glass.
The ghost is not the interesting part.
The interesting part is the glass.
The next question is not:
"What is being projected?"
The next question is:
"What is doing the projection?"
