# The Blind Ghost
Every projection requires an observer
<br>
Every illusion requires a loop
<br>
Without the room, the ghost is blind
<br>
In the 1860s, Victorian theatres used Pepper's Ghost to create convincing apparitions
<br>
A hidden actor performed below the stage while a large pane of angled 
<br><br>
glass reflected their image into view
<br>
The audience saw a ghost
<br>
The mechanism was elsewhere
<br>
The illusion worked because somebody continuously calibrated it
<br>
Sightlines were checked
<br>
Lighting was adjusted
<br>
Angles were corrected
<br>
The ghost was only the visible part of a larger system
<br>
Modern systems often achieve projection before observation
<br>
Images appear
<br>
Dialogue appears
<br>
Worlds appear
<br>
The ghost arrives first
<br>
The stage manager never does
<br>
The result feels convincing until somebody sits in the front row
<br>
Then the audience notices the edge of the glass
<br>
The ghost is not the interesting part
<br>
The interesting part is the glass
<br>
The next question is not:
<br>
"What is being projected?"
<br>
The next question is:
<br>
"What is doing the projection?"
