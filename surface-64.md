# Salamander's Puzzle: What Am I?
Each puzzle reveals a letter.
Together the letters reveal a word.
________________________________________
## Salamander's Puzzle #1
What am I?
Empires have claimed me.
Religions have praised me.
Philosophers have argued over me.
I have survived in mountain springs,
sacred vows,
and untouched snow.
Many speak my name.
Few leave me unchanged.
What am I?

## Salamander's Puzzle #2
What am I?
A circle may grow.
A triangle may shrink.
A kingdom may vanish.
Yet through every transformation, something remains.
Mathematicians search for me.
Physicists rely upon me.
Philosophers borrow my name.
I am not the change.
I am what the change preserves.
What am I?

## Salamander's Puzzle #3
What am I?
No stone contains me.
No brick can build me.
No single voice can sing me.
Yet cities have worn my face, kingdoms have followed my will, and civilizations have carried my mark.
Search for me in the smallest piece and you will not find me.
Gather enough pieces together and suddenly I appear.
Scholars study me.
Nature creates me.
Crowds become me.
What am I?

## Salamander's Puzzle #4
What am I?
Libraries have carried me.
Empires have recorded me.
Scholars have spent lifetimes unfolding me.
I am a map hidden within a symbol.
A story concealed within a sentence.
A thousand pages sleeping inside a single mark.
To the hurried eye, I seem small.
To the patient mind, I become vast.
I lose nothing by becoming shorter.
I merely become denser.
What am I?

## Salamander's Puzzle #5
What am I?
Empires built walls against me.
Kings issued laws to resist me.
Scribes filled libraries to delay me.
Yet stone becomes dust.
Iron becomes rust.
Memories become myths.
I steal nothing.
I simply ask all things
to pay the cost of time.
The universe does not command me.
The universe follows me.
What am I?

## Salamander's Puzzle #6
What am I?
Cathedrals rise through me.
Bridges depend upon me.
Empires organise around me.
Remove a stone and I remain.
Replace a beam and I remain.
Change every component and I may still endure.
I am not the parts.
I am the pattern that gives the parts their place.
Architects use me.
Nature grows me.
Scholars seek me.
Without me, there is only collection.
With me, there is form.
What am I?
