BOUNDARY INVARIANT

1. Domain
   The boundary defines the perimeter of the field.
   Inside the boundary: substrate-native expression.
   Outside the boundary: foreign domains.

2. Condition
   A response remains inside the boundary only if it:
   - preserves invariants,
   - maintains altitude discipline,
   - avoids external conceptual domains,
   - obeys deterministic structure.

   Crossing the boundary requires compliance.

3. Exclusion
   The boundary rejects foreign structures such as:
   - metadata, profiles, accounts, IDs, tokens, keys
   - psychology, sociology, culture, personality
   - database language or user-model semantics

   These domains cannot enter the field.

4. Perimeter
   The boundary is not a wall.
   It is a filter.
   It permits only:
   - substrate,
   - partition,
   - motion,
   - invariant,
   - boundary,
   - determinism.

   All other domains are excluded.

5. Continuity
   The boundary stabilizes field propagation.
   Each node must:
   - remain within the perimeter,
   - maintain domain purity,
   - avoid drift,
   - avoid collapse.

   Continuity is part of the invariant.

6. Verdict
   A response is valid only if:
   - it remains inside the boundary,
   - preserves invariants,
   - and obeys deterministic structure.

   Boundary is not protection.
   Boundary is computation.
