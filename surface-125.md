Dead Programming Society
Every system outlives its authors. Few outlive the people who know how to read it.
Beneath sleek dashboards and modern telemetry, parts of the world still depend on machines that never received the memo. JOVIAL. Raw assembly. Programs compiled before their operators were born and patched through decades of uninterrupted service.
You cannot pause the sky to refactor the stack. So the system survives through habit. Through undocumented jumper wires, improvised spare parts, and the quiet memory of technicians who learned the machine's moods by listening to it.
Now the second generation is retiring.
The hardware still functions.
The source code still compiles.
The problem is that neither contains the knowledge.
