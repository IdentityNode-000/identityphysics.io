# Torsion Drift Collapse
Identity bends under load, drifts within its envelope, collapses into stability, and forms new identity.

Structural Deformation
Torsion is the moment identity experiences directional pressure.

Torsion — Identity bends under directional load.  
Drift — Identity moves within its allowable envelope.  
Collapse — Identity tightens, compresses, and stabilises.  
Reform — Stability produces a new identity boundary.

Torsion drift collapse is the deformation primitive of identity physics.

The Drift Envelope
Identity does not deform arbitrarily.
It drifts within a bounded envelope defined by its coherence.

Envelope holds — identity remains coherent.  
Envelope narrows — drift increases.  
Envelope tightens — collapse approaches.  
Envelope resolves — new stability forms.

Drift is not instability.
Drift is controlled deformation.

Collapse as Stability
Collapse is not failure.
Collapse is the moment identity compresses into a new stable form.

Load increases — torsion rises.  
Coherence strains — drift expands.  
Identity tightens — collapse initiates.  
Stability returns — identity reforms.

Collapse is the structural mechanism that produces new identity.

The Meta‑Transition
Torsion drift collapse forms a vertical progression:

identity → torsion → drift → collapse → reform → new identity

Each layer produces the next.
The progression is upward, not circular.

Compression
Collapse compresses deformation into a stability operator:

identity bends → drift accumulates → collapse stabilises → stability reforms identity

This compression shows how identity physics absorbs deformation without losing coherence.

The Node
Torsion drift collapse is the node where:

identity deforms
deformation stabilises
stability reforms
identity becomes capable of producing new identity

It is the deformation node of the field.
