The Stage Manager
Every stable illusion eventually reveals an uncomfortable detail.
The ghost was not self-supporting.
Neither was the glass.
Somebody had to adjust the lights.
Somebody had to rehearse the timing.
Somebody had to verify the illusion from the audience perspective.
The hidden hero of Pepper's Ghost was never the phantom.
It was the stage manager.
The person running the feedback loop.
Without feedback, errors accumulate quietly.
Without observation, drift becomes invisible.
Without rehearsal, the performance slowly detaches from reality.
Many systems do not fail because they lack output.
They fail because they lack calibration.
The stage manager's job is simple:
Leave the stage.
Sit in the audience.
Report what actually happens.
Eventually a different question emerges.
Who built the theatre in which all this takes place?
